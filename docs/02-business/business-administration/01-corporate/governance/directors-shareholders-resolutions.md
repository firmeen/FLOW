# Directors, Shareholders, Resolutions and Corporate Authority

## Why this document family exists
A company is a legal person, but it acts through directors, shareholders, employees, and authorized representatives. FLOW therefore needs evidence showing who had authority to make a decision, sign a contract, open a bank account, issue shares, borrow money, approve major spending, or change the company's registered particulars.

## Document families

### Director / board records
Use for decisions within director authority. Typical evidence may include:
- board meeting notice where applicable;
- agenda;
- attendance record;
- board minutes;
- written resolution where legally and procedurally appropriate;
- supporting papers;
- signed resolution/minutes;
- execution evidence for the approved action.

### Shareholder records
Used where a matter requires shareholder action or where ownership records change. Typical documents include:
- meeting notice;
- proxy documentation where used;
- attendance/quorum evidence;
- minutes/resolution;
- updated shareholder register/list;
- share certificate or transfer documentation as applicable;
- filing evidence for changes that must be registered.

## Authority matrix
FLOW should maintain a practical authority table describing who can:
- sign ordinary customer SaaS agreements;
- approve discounts above thresholds;
- approve vendor contracts;
- approve outgoing payments;
- open/close bank accounts;
- appoint bank users;
- approve refunds above thresholds;
- hire employees;
- sign employment/contractor agreements;
- sign DPAs/security schedules;
- approve capital/share changes;
- approve borrowing/guarantees;
- approve related-party transactions.

The authority matrix is an internal control and must remain consistent with the company's legally registered director/signing authority.

## Example flow: opening a new bank account

```text
Finance identifies need
→ verify company signing authority
→ prepare board/director approval if bank requires it
→ resolution signed
→ prepare current corporate KYC documents
→ submit to bank
→ bank opens account
→ establish users/approval limits
→ record account in treasury register
→ accounting system updated
→ payment/reconciliation controls activated
→ resolution + application + account evidence archived together
```

## Example flow: capital increase

```text
Funding need / investment terms
→ lawyer/accountant reviews structure
→ required corporate approvals
→ subscription/share documentation
→ payment for shares
→ statutory/register updates
→ required DBD filings
→ new corporate documents
→ cap table update
→ accounting entry
→ investor/shareholder file update
```

Do not update the cap table first and treat it as proof that shares legally changed hands.

## Related-party transactions
If a founder, director, shareholder, or related company supplies services, lends money, advances expenses, or enters another transaction with FLOW, keep stronger documentation:
- nature of relationship;
- commercial purpose;
- agreement;
- pricing/basis;
- approval/conflict handling;
- invoice/payment evidence;
- accounting/tax treatment.

This prevents personal and company funds from becoming indistinguishable.

## Common control failures
- director signs outside registered authority;
- board approval is prepared after the transaction only to fill a gap;
- shareholder records and cap table disagree;
- old authority remains active at a bank after director changes;
- former employee retains payment/admin approval rights;
- material decisions exist only in chat.
