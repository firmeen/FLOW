# Founders, Equity and Investment Documents

## Purpose
This document family explains how ownership, founder obligations, share issuance, investment, and later dilution should be evidenced. These documents answer three different questions that must not be mixed:

1. Who is building and committing to the company?
2. Who legally owns shares today?
3. What rights attach to those shares or investment instruments?

## Founder stage
Use a Founder Agreement before or during incorporation when multiple people are jointly creating FLOW or when equity is promised for future contribution.

Key areas:
- founder roles and expected time commitment;
- cash/assets contributed;
- pre-incorporation IP and its transfer;
- initial ownership intention;
- vesting or earning conditions if used;
- founder departure scenarios;
- decision rights;
- confidentiality;
- conflicts and outside projects;
- what happens if the company is never incorporated.

A Founder Agreement is not proof of registered share ownership. After incorporation, ownership must be reflected in the company's proper shareholder records and any required legal documentation.

## Shareholder stage
A Shareholders' Agreement becomes useful when the relationship requires governance beyond ordinary company law/statutory documents.

It may address:
- reserved matters;
- board appointment;
- information rights;
- transfer restrictions;
- rights of first refusal/pre-emption;
- new share issues and dilution;
- tag/drag arrangements where suitable;
- founder restrictions/commitments;
- investor protections;
- deadlock;
- exit mechanics;
- confidentiality.

## Cap table operating rule
The cap table is an analytical management view. It must reconcile to legal share records.

Every change should map to evidence:

```text
Cap-table movement
→ legal event
→ approval
→ subscription/transfer/instrument documents
→ payment evidence if applicable
→ statutory record/filing update
→ accounting treatment
→ revised cap table
```

## Investment due-diligence pack
Before an investment round, FLOW should be able to assemble:
- current incorporation documents;
- director/shareholder records;
- cap table;
- founder/shareholder agreements;
- material customer contracts;
- material vendor contracts;
- IP ownership chain;
- employment/contractor IP documents;
- financial statements/management accounts;
- tax filings/status;
- bank/debt records;
- litigation/dispute summary if any;
- privacy/security documentation;
- open-source register;
- material licenses/permits.

## Investment closing flow

```text
term negotiation
→ legal structure chosen
→ due diligence
→ investment/share subscription documents drafted
→ board/shareholder approvals
→ signing
→ investor funds received
→ statutory/corporate updates
→ accounting entry
→ cap table update
→ certificates/registers/filings updated
→ closing set archived
```

The closing set should preserve every signed document and proof that all required closing conditions were completed.

## Founder advances and shareholder loans
When a founder pays company costs personally or lends money to FLOW, record the transaction clearly rather than treating all founder money as informal equity.

Keep:
- expense/payment evidence;
- purpose;
- whether reimbursable expense, loan, capital contribution, or other treatment;
- approval;
- repayment terms if a loan;
- accounting entry;
- tax/legal review where material.

## Common failures
- promising equity in chat with no legal follow-through;
- cap table differs from company register;
- founder-created code never transferred;
- personal expenses and company funding mixed together;
- investor receives funds/shares before required approvals are completed;
- no clean closing binder after funding.
