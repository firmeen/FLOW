# Company Formation and Registration

## Objective
Explain the document chain for creating the legal entity that will own, operate, contract, invoice, hire, pay tax, and hold FLOW's intellectual property.

## 1. Before registration
FLOW should decide and document:
- intended company name and alternatives;
- founders/promoters/shareholders;
- registered capital structure;
- initial share allocation;
- directors;
- director signing authority;
- registered office;
- business objectives broad enough to cover software/SaaS, technology services, implementation, consulting, data processing, integrations, and related lawful activities;
- whether assets created before incorporation will be transferred to the company.

These decisions become inputs to registration documents. Do not treat them as informal notes only.

## 2. Registration pack
Maintain the complete incorporation pack produced/filed through the applicable DBD process, together with evidence supporting registered office and any required consents.

Store two views:

```text
CURRENT/
  latest certificate
  latest directors/signing authority
  latest registered address
  latest shareholder information
  latest memorandum/objectives

HISTORY/
  incorporation pack
  every later amendment
  superseded certificates
  filings and receipts
```

## 3. After incorporation: immediate handoff
Registration is not the end. It triggers a sequence:

```text
Company legally formed
→ obtain current certified/company documents
→ establish accounting books and accountant relationship
→ open corporate bank account
→ establish tax status/registrations
→ transfer founder-owned IP/assets where required
→ migrate business subscriptions/accounts where practical
→ set customer/vendor contracting entity
→ configure invoices/receipts
→ configure payment-provider merchant/KYC identity
→ issue internal authorization/resolutions
```

## 4. Contacts and parties
Typical parties involved:
- DBD / corporate registration channel;
- company director(s);
- shareholders/founders;
- accountant/accounting firm;
- lawyer/corporate adviser where needed;
- bank;
- Revenue Department/tax adviser;
- payment providers;
- landlord/office provider if address evidence is needed.

## 5. Common failures
- contracts use founder name while invoices use company name;
- domain/code remains personally owned with no transfer evidence;
- bank/payment provider is opened under inconsistent identity;
- old certificate is repeatedly submitted after director/address changes;
- company objectives are too narrow for actual services;
- nobody retains incorporation evidence after online registration is completed.

## 6. Event-driven changes after formation
Create a corporate-change file whenever there is:
- company name change;
- registered office change;
- director/signing authority change;
- capital change;
- shareholder change;
- branch registration/change;
- business objective amendment.

Each event should connect approval/resolution → filing → new current certificate/record → updates to bank, tax, contracts, invoices, payment providers, privacy notices, and customer/vendor KYC where relevant.
